Music learning · Android

Learn piano and guitar on your phone

New to music? Start with a few notes on your Android phone, practise a simple rhythm, then build towards playing a physical piano or guitar. Melody Garden offers offline lessons and on-screen practice; microphone feedback is optional.

How to start learning music on Android

  1. Choose one instrument. Start with piano note locations or guitar string names instead of switching between both during a short session.
  2. Play a small pattern. For piano, find the white C key just before two black keys, then try C-D-E-D-C slowly. For guitar, learn the open string names before trying more notes.
  3. Repeat without rushing. Keep an even count, notice the part you find difficult, and repeat that part before increasing speed.
  4. Check what works offline. Open an installed lesson, switch off the connection and repeat the practice you intend to use. Do this before relying on an app while travelling.

A phone can teach note names, rhythm patterns and practice habits. Touchscreens cannot reproduce the feel of physical keys, string pressure or playing technique, so practise on an instrument when one is available.

Online keyboard or offline Android lessons?

An online keyboard is useful for tapping notes in a browser without installing anything. A dedicated Android learning app is more suitable when you want a lesson sequence and local progress, including while offline after installation.

With Melody Garden, you can choose piano or guitar and practise on screen without granting microphone permission. Optional microphone-based pitch feedback and a guitar tuner use live audio on the device. Progress is stored locally, not synced through an account.
