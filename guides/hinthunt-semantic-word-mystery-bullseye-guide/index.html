By Wonderworld Labs · Published 2026-11-12

In HintHunt, every guess is measured by its semantic distance to the secret word. Start with distinct conceptual domains (nature, objects, emotions) to locate the warm ring quickly, then zoom into synonyms and close associates to land the bullseye.

How semantic distance works in HintHunt

  1. Understand that HintHunt evaluates meaning and contextual relationships, not alphabetical similarity.
  2. Throw your first three darts into divergent conceptual categories: living things, human-made artifacts, and abstract ideas.
  3. Once a category enters the inner rings or triggers Orbit's 'WARMER' status, discard the other categories.

Powered by trained MiniLM semantic embeddings computed privately on your device, HintHunt positions words in high-dimensional vector space. Words that frequently share contexts score closer to the center bullseye.

Triangulating from outer rings to the bullseye

If 'ANIMAL' lands in the middle ring, test sub-classes like 'MAMMAL', 'OCEAN', or 'BIRD'. When 'OCEAN' pushes you into the inner ring, aquatic animals like 'WHALE', 'SHARK', or 'DOLPHIN' rapidly converge on the secret.

Managing your 10 darts for maximum information gain

Avoid submitting minor spelling variants or plurals early. Each dart should deliver broad information until you are within the inner yellow ring.

Questions and answers

Does HintHunt send my guesses to an external server?

No. The semantic embedding model runs completely on-device in your browser using local vector math.

Are synonyms always closer than antonyms?

Generally yes, but strongly linked antonym pairs (hot/cold) can share semantic contexts. Orbit's directional clues help guide the distinction.

Can I play past daily secret words?

HintHunt offers a new puzzle every midnight local time, with solved records saved in your browser history.
