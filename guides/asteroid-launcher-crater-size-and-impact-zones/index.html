Asteroid Launcher: How Crater Size and Impact Zones are Calculated

By Wonderworld Labs · Published 2026-11-14

Crater size and impact damage scale primarily with kinetic energy, impact velocity, and trajectory angle. Shallow angles transfer more energy into atmospheric airbursts, while steep impacts maximize direct crater excavation and ground shockwaves.

Kinetic energy and diameter scaling

In the simulation, kinetic energy scales with projectile mass and the square of entry velocity. Doubling the diameter of an iron or stone asteroid increases mass eightfold, dramatically widening the initial cavity diameter and vaporization zone.

Impact angles and atmospheric disruption

Steep 90-degree impacts direct kinetic energy straight into the ground, maximizing crater depth and central peak uplift. In contrast, shallow 20-to-45-degree angles prolong atmospheric transit, increasing the fireball envelope and thermal radiation zone.

Reading the tiered destruction zones

Check the map rings outward from Ground Zero: the vaporized excavation rim, the thermal radiation perimeter where combustible materials ignite, and the atmospheric shockwave boundary where overpressure causes structural damage.

Questions and answers

Why does asteroid composition change the crater size?

Dense iron and gold asteroids experience less atmospheric ablation and retain higher terminal velocity at ground level compared to porous comet or carbonaceous stone projectiles.
