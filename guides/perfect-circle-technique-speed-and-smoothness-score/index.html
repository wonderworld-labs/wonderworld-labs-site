Draw a Perfect Circle: Balancing Speed, Radius and Smoothness

By Wonderworld Labs · Published 2026-11-14

Achieve high scores by pivoting smoothly from your elbow or shoulder rather than your wrist, maintaining a steady moderate drawing speed, and closing the perimeter loop without overshooting the start coordinate.

Pivot from the elbow instead of the wrist

The human wrist has an elliptical range of motion that introduces flattening on top and bottom arcs. By anchoring your elbow and rotating your entire forearm, you trace a natural circular sweep with consistent radial distance.

Avoid hesitation and jerky speed changes

The grading algorithm evaluates both perimeter deviation and contour smoothness. Drawing too slowly creates micro-jitters, while drawing too fast leads to angular drift. Aim for a confident single-stroke completion in 1.5 to 2.5 seconds.

Focus on the closure seam

A common score penalty comes from overlapping the start point or leaving an open gap. Watch the starting coordinate as you round the final quadrant and feather your finger gently to meet the initial pixel.

Questions and answers

What score threshold unlocks the Smooth bonus?

Consistently maintaining less than 3% variance in radial distance while completing the loop in under 3 seconds earns the Smooth evaluation badge and scores above 90%.
