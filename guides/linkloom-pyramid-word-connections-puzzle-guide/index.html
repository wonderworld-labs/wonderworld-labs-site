Linkloom Pyramid Guide: Solving Compound Word Connections

By Wonderworld Labs · Published 2026-11-12

Solving Linkloom compound word pyramids begins with finding anchor words that only pair with one other tile on the board, establishing the top and bottom tiers first, and saving check attempts until the full pyramid structure feels cohesive.

Deconstructing the phrase pyramid structure

  1. Notice that each parent tile connected by dotted lines to a child tile must form a recognized compound word or familiar phrase.
  2. Check both directions: Parent + Child (e.g., GREEN + BERRY) or Child + Parent.
  3. Begin with tiles that have strict grammatical pairings, leaving flexible words (like FIRE, WOOD, BACK) for intermediate positions.

A 6-tile pyramid contains multiple overlapping connections. A word placed incorrectly in the middle tier breaks both its parent link above and its child links below.

Identifying anchor tiles with solitary pairings

Scan the board for unique nouns or adjectives. If a word like 'BERRY' can only form a legitimate phrase with 'GREEN', lock that connection early to eliminate variables.

Conserving checks and using the undo buffer

You are granted 4 check attempts per puzzle. Swapping tiles is unlimited and completely free, so experiment with candidate layouts before consuming a validation check.

Questions and answers

Do compound words need to be single words or two-word phrases?

Both single closed compounds (e.g., FIREWOOD) and standard open phrases (e.g., GREEN TEA) are valid.

What happens if I use all 4 checks?

The game shows your current correct connection count, allowing you to reshuffle or continue deducing.

Are there other game modes besides phrases?

Yes, Linkloom also features Numbers mode (arithmetic pyramid sums) and Fragments mode.
