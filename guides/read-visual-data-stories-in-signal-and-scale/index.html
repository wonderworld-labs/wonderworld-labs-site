How to Read a Data Story in Signal & Scale

By Wonderworld Labs · Published 2026-11-14

Open a Signal & Scale story and pick one chart. Read what the chart measures, check the unit and date, then follow its references. Use those details to decide whether two values can be compared.

Check the labels before comparing the bars or lines

  1. Read the chart title and identify the population, place or group it covers.
  2. Check the unit. A total, a percentage and a rate per person answer different questions.
  3. Check the dates. Note whether a chart shows a single year or a period and whether both values come from the same time.
  4. Open the reference supplied with the story. Look for the definitions or notes that explain the figures.

Use one comparison to test your reading

Suppose an aging-population chart shows the share of residents above a specified age. A place with a larger share can still have fewer people in that age group if its total population is smaller. Read the age cutoff, the denominator and the year before deciding what the comparison says.

Write your interpretation in a sentence naming the metric, the places and the date. If the chart leaves one of these unclear, check the source before repeating the comparison.

Keep the chart date with the claim

Signal & Scale stories are dated snapshots. Save the story link and its date with any notes you take. If you need a newer figure, look for it in the linked source and check whether the measurement is still defined the same way.

If two figures differ, check for a revised estimate, a different reporting period or a different group before assuming either figure is wrong.

Questions and answers

Where can I check the source for a chart?

Open its story and look for the references and dates supplied alongside it.

Is a bigger proportion the same as a bigger total?

The total also depends on the size of the group. Compare proportions with proportions, and check the denominator if you need counts.

Are the charts a live feed?

The stories are dated snapshots. Check the date and references before using a figure.
