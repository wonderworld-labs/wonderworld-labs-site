Absurd Trolley Problems: Navigating Moral Dilemmas and Community Consensus

By Wonderworld Labs · Published 2026-11-14

Each scenario forces a choice between passive consequence and active intervention. Analyze whether your instinct favors saving the maximum number of lives or avoiding direct moral culpability, then compare your verdict against the percentage of other players.

The classic five-versus-one switch

The baseline scenario tests classic utilitarian ethics: pulling the switch sacrifices one worker on the sidetrack to spare five on the main track. Over 70% of global players consistently choose to pull the lever in this pure numerical trade-off.

Factoring identity and absurd complications

As rounds progress, the game introduces sentient robots, your worst enemy, historical figures, clones, and inanimate objects like your original Mona Lisa painting. These factors test whether your moral calculus shifts when personal affection or identity is at stake.

Reviewing your end-of-run moral summary

At the end of a round set, the game tabulates how many total lives you spared, how many times you pulled the lever, and where your ethics align with or diverge from the global player majority.

Questions and answers

Does the game save your statistics across sessions?

Player choices contribute anonymously to global crowd statistics and your personal scorecard is displayed at the end of each completed run in your browser.
